The Spray Polyurethane Foam Alliance's National Industry Excellence Awards is one of the longest-running recognition programs in the spray foam trade — a real, established program, not a marketing invention. Here's how it actually works.

## A Peer-Judged Submission Program

Unlike the live Top Gun competition, the SPFA excellence awards judge completed projects submitted by contractors and companies — insulation, roofing, and coatings applications entered for consideration across defined project categories. Entries are reviewed and judged by industry peers on technical execution and application quality.

## Categories Reflect the Trade's Real Scope

The program spans the range of real spray polyurethane foam applications: residential and commercial insulation, roofing applications, and elastomeric coatings work. This breadth matters — it means winning recognition in one category doesn't automatically imply expertise across every application type, since roofing and residential insulation involve genuinely different technical skill sets.

## What Winning Actually Signals

A win reflects that a specific completed project, submitted and reviewed at a specific point in time, met a peer-reviewed bar for technical quality. It's a real, earned recognition — but like any awards program judging past work, it's a snapshot, not a permanent certification. The team, equipment, and quality control on today's job may or may not match the submitted project from a prior year.

## Why the Program's Longevity Matters

A recognition program that's run consistently for well over a decade, with real industry participation year over year, carries more weight than a one-off award — it reflects sustained peer engagement with the standard being judged, not a single marketing push.

## Using This Information as a Homeowner

If a contractor mentions an SPFA excellence award, it's reasonable — and easy — to ask which year, which category, and which specific project won. A specific, verifiable answer is a good sign. Beyond that, pair it with the more directly relevant, currently-checkable signals covered elsewhere on this site: manufacturer certification and license verification.
