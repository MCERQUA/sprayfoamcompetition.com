You don't need to be a competition judge to spot a below-standard spray foam job. Here are five specific, checkable signs.

## 1. Visible Thickness Variation

Look across a wall cavity or attic surface for spots where the foam clearly looks thinner or thicker than the surrounding area. Judges measure this precisely; you can often spot the more obvious cases just by looking closely, especially near corners and edges.

## 2. Gaps at Corners and Penetrations

Corners, framing joints, and areas around pipes or wiring penetrations are the hardest spots to cover completely, and they're the most common place to find missed coverage. These gaps directly undercut the air-sealing benefit that's a core part of what you're paying for.

## 3. A Persistent Chemical Smell

A mild smell for the first day or two after installation is normal curing off-gas. A strong odor that lingers well beyond that window is a real signal of off-ratio mixing — a genuine application-quality failure, not a normal part of the process.

## 4. Foam Pulling Away From Framing

Shrinkage or visible gaps between the cured foam and the framing members, sometimes appearing weeks after installation, is a classic sign of improperly mixed foam. It's not something that shows up on installation day, which is part of why it's worth a follow-up inspection a few weeks after the job.

## 5. Overspray on Surfaces That Shouldn't Have Any

Foam residue on trim, flooring, or fixtures that weren't properly masked is a visible proxy for how carefully — or carelessly — the crew approached the whole job, even though it doesn't directly affect insulation performance.

## What to Do If You Spot One

Any single item here might have a reasonable explanation. Document what you see with photos and raise it directly with the installer — a professional operation will investigate and address a legitimate quality concern, while resistance to even discussing it is itself a signal worth taking seriously.
